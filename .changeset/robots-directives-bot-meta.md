`access-crawl-control/robots-directives` and `answer-readiness/snippet-gate-coverage` now read robots directives only under the names a crawler operator documents: `robots`, `googlebot`, `googlebot-news`, `bingbot` and `applebot` (verified 2026-10-20). The list is shared in one module. A directive addressed to `gptbot`, `oai-searchbot`, `chatgpt-user`, `claudebot`, `claude-searchbot`, `claude-user`, `perplexitybot`, `ccbot`, `google-extended`, `applebot-extended` or `msnbot`, in a meta tag or a bot-scoped `X-Robots-Tag`, no longer fails or warns either audit. robots-directives names such a tag in its message. snippet-gate-coverage lists it in `found` as not evaluated. A bot prefix in `X-Robots-Tag` now scopes every directive after it, so `gptbot: noindex, nosnippet` binds gptbot only. snippet-gate-coverage now evaluates `applebot`, which Apple documents. Sites that addressed a directive only to an undocumented name may see their score rise. `access-crawl-control/no-nofollow` uses the same names and header parser. `X-Robots-Tag: gptbot: nofollow` and `<meta name="gptbot" content="nofollow">` no longer count as nofollow for every crawler. It reads every robots meta tag rather than the last one, and also counts `nofollow` addressed to `googlebot`, `bingbot` or `applebot`.
